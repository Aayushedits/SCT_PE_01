# Task 2: Prompting for Creativity
**Internship:** SkillCraft Technology  
**Project theme:** Startup ideas for college students in India

## 1. Objective

The objective of this task is to explore how changes in prompt wording influence AI-generated creative content. Four prompt versions were used: a base prompt and three variations that emphasize professional tone, creativity, and detailed output. The outputs were reviewed for tone, creativity, detail, and practicality.

## 2. Base Prompt

> Generate 3 innovative startup ideas that solve real problems faced by college students in India. For each idea, provide a name, the problem it solves, the target audience, its unique selling point, and a simple monetization strategy. Keep the ideas practical, creative, and suitable for a student founder with a limited budget.

### Summary of the generated output

1. **CampusLoop — Student-to-Student Marketplace:** A campus-focused marketplace for buying, selling, renting, and exchanging used academic supplies and student essentials. Possible revenue sources include transaction commissions, featured listings, and local shop partnerships.
2. **SkillSwap — Learn by Teaching:** A peer-to-peer skill exchange where students teach one another skills such as coding, design, video editing, and communication. Possible revenue sources include premium memberships, workshops, and mentoring.
3. **MessMate — Affordable Student Meal Planning:** A service that suggests affordable meals and grocery lists based on a student's budget, preferences, ingredients, and kitchen equipment. Possible revenue sources include premium meal plans and grocery partnerships.

## 3. Experiment A — Professional Tone

### Prompt used

> Generate 3 startup ideas that solve real problems faced by college students in India. Use a professional and business-oriented tone. For each idea, explain the problem statement, proposed solution, target market, competitive advantage, and revenue model. Prioritize feasibility and market relevance for a student founder with a limited budget.

### Summary of the generated output

The response presented CampusLoop, SkillSwap, and MessMate using consistent business-oriented sections: problem statement, proposed solution, target market, competitive advantage, revenue model, and feasibility. It emphasized market relevance and low-cost validation.

### Initial observation

The explicit structure and professional-tone instruction made the output more formal and business-focused than the base prompt.

## 4. Experiment B — Creative Tone

### Prompt used

> Act as a highly creative startup innovator. Generate 3 original and unconventional startup ideas that solve real problems faced by college students in India. Give each idea a memorable name, a surprising solution, a unique selling point, and a practical revenue model. Avoid generic ideas while keeping them achievable for a student founder with a limited budget.

### Summary of the generated output

1. **CampusLoop — The Circular Campus Economy:** A semester-based reuse network for selling, renting, and exchanging campus items.
2. **SkillSpark — The 30-Minute Skill Swap:** Short peer-learning sessions where students earn credits by teaching skills and use credits to learn from others.
3. **Leftover Lab — Smart Shared Grocery Planning:** A shared grocery and meal-planning concept that helps roommates use ingredients they already have and avoid duplicate purchases.

### Initial observation

The creative role, memorable naming, and instruction to avoid generic ideas encouraged more distinctive framing and mechanisms. The ideas still aimed to remain feasible for a student founder.

## 5. Experiment C — Detailed and Example-Driven Prompt

### Prompt used

> Generate 3 startup ideas that solve real problems faced by college students in India. For each idea, explain the problem, proposed solution, target audience, unique selling point, a realistic example of how a student would use it, estimated initial costs with assumptions, and a potential revenue model. Provide specific and actionable details while keeping each idea feasible for a student founder with a limited budget.

### Summary of the generated output

1. **CampusLoop — Student Marketplace:** Includes a student-to-student listing and exchange workflow, an example transaction, and illustrative initial cost estimates of ₹1,300–₹4,200.
2. **SkillSpark — The 30-Minute Skill Swap:** Includes short peer-learning sessions, an example of exchanging a video-editing lesson for another skill, and illustrative initial cost estimates of ₹300–₹2,800.
3. **Leftover Lab — Smart Shared Grocery Planning:** Includes shared ingredient tracking and shopping lists, an example involving roommates, and illustrative initial cost estimates of ₹300–₹2,800.

### Initial observation

The request for examples, initial costs, assumptions, and revenue models produced more implementation-focused responses. Cost figures are illustrative planning estimates, not verified market prices.

## 6. Comparative Evaluation

Use a scale of 1 to 5, where 1 = poor and 5 = excellent. The following are **initial qualitative ratings based on the outputs generated in this exercise**. They are not measurements from a formal user study.

| Criterion | Base Prompt | Experiment A: Professional | Experiment B: Creative | Experiment C: Detailed |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| Tone matches intended style | 3 | 5 | 5 | 4 |
| Creativity / originality | 3 | 3 | 5 | 3 |
| Level of detail | 3 | 4 | 3 | 5 |
| Practicality for a student founder | 4 | 4 | 4 | 5 |

### Interpretation

- **Base prompt:** Provides a useful general baseline and covers the requested core fields.
- **Experiment A:** Improves consistency and business-oriented framing by explicitly defining the information required for each idea.
- **Experiment B:** Encourages more distinctive concepts and names, though originality should still be judged against real alternatives and user needs.
- **Experiment C:** Provides the most implementation-oriented output because it explicitly asks for examples, estimated costs, and assumptions.

## 7. Key Observations

1. **Specific instructions improve structure.** Listing the required sections helps the response cover each requested dimension consistently.
2. **Tone instructions affect presentation.** Words such as “professional and business-oriented” encourage formal language, while “highly creative” encourages more imaginative framing.
3. **Creativity instructions can change the concepts.** Asking for unconventional solutions and memorable names led to different ideas and more distinctive approaches.
4. **Examples and assumptions increase actionability.** Requesting a realistic user scenario and estimated initial costs makes the output easier to evaluate and potentially test.
5. **Prompt quality depends on the goal.** A professional prompt is useful for business presentation, a creative prompt is useful for ideation, and a detailed prompt is useful for planning.
6. **AI outputs need validation.** Cost estimates and business assumptions should be checked before being used in a real startup plan.

## 8. Conclusion

This experiment showed that prompt wording influences the style, structure, originality, and actionability of AI-generated startup ideas. Experiment A was strongest for formal business presentation, Experiment B emphasized creative framing, and Experiment C produced the most detailed implementation guidance. There is no single best prompt for every purpose; the most effective prompt depends on the desired outcome.

For an early-stage student founder, a useful next step would be to combine the strengths of these variations: request original ideas, specify a clear structure, and ask for examples and assumptions. The resulting ideas should then be validated with actual students before making business decisions.

## 9. Limitations

- The evaluation is qualitative and based on a small number of generated responses.
- Ratings are subjective and may vary by evaluator.
- Initial cost estimates are illustrative, not verified quotes.
- The ideas have not been validated through surveys, interviews, or market testing.
