## Objective

This experiment explores how changing prompt wording affects AI-generated startup ideas. Outputs are reviewed for tone, creativity, detail, and practicality.

## Base Prompt

### Prompt

> Generate 3 innovative startup ideas that solve real problems faced by college students in India. For each idea, provide a name, the problem it solves, the target audience, its unique selling point, and a simple monetization strategy. Keep the ideas practical, creative, and suitable for a student founder with a limited budget.

### Generated output

**1. CampusLoop — Campus Marketplace for Affordable Student Essentials**

- **Problem:** College students often need textbooks, calculators, lab equipment, and other academic essentials but may not want to buy them at full price.
- **Solution:** A campus-focused marketplace where students can buy, sell, rent, or exchange used academic supplies and other student essentials.
- **Target Audience:** College students, particularly first-year students, senior students, and students living in hostels or rented accommodation.
- **Unique Selling Point:** CampusLoop focuses on college communities, making it easier for students to find affordable items from other students nearby.
- **Monetization Strategy:** Earn commissions on completed transactions, offer paid featured listings, and collaborate with local stationery and printing businesses.

**2. SkillSwap — Learn and Teach Skills Within Your Campus**

- **Problem:** Many college students want to learn practical skills such as programming, graphic design, video editing, and communication, but paid courses can be expensive.
- **Solution:** A peer-to-peer platform where students exchange skills. For example, a student can teach basic web development in return for a video-editing lesson.
- **Target Audience:** College students interested in affordable skill development and peer learning.
- **Unique Selling Point:** Students can learn new skills by sharing their existing knowledge rather than paying for every lesson.
- **Monetization Strategy:** Generate revenue through premium memberships, paid workshops, and optional expert mentoring sessions.

**3. MessMate — Budget-Friendly Meal Planning for Students**

- **Problem:** Students living away from home often struggle to manage food expenses, cooking time, grocery shopping, and affordable meals.
- **Solution:** A meal-planning platform that suggests weekly meals based on a student's budget, food preferences, available ingredients, and kitchen equipment.
- **Target Audience:** College students living in rented flats, paying guest accommodation, and hostels with limited food options.
- **Unique Selling Point:** MessMate focuses on student budgets and practical cooking constraints rather than general-purpose meal planning.
- **Monetization Strategy:** Offer premium personalized meal plans, grocery affiliate commissions, and partnerships with local grocery stores.

**Observation:**

The base prompt generated three practical startup ideas addressing common college-student problems. Each idea included a problem, target audience, unique selling point, and monetization strategy. The ideas were feasible for a student founder, although additional implementation details could improve their originality and actionability.

## Experiment B - Creative Tone

### Prompt

> Act as a highly creative startup innovator. Generate 3 original and unconventional startup ideas that solve real problems faced by college students in India. Give each idea a memorable name, a surprising solution, a unique selling point, and a practical revenue model. Avoid generic ideas while keeping them achievable for a student founder with a limited budget.

### Generated output

EXPERIMENT B — CREATIVE TONE

**1. CampusLoop — The Circular Campus Economy**

- **Problem:** College students often have unused textbooks, lab coats, calculators, and other useful items, while other students spend money buying the same things.
- **Surprising Solution:** A campus-only marketplace where students can exchange, rent, sell, or donate items within their college community.
- **Unique Selling Point:** Instead of competing with general shopping platforms, CampusLoop builds trust through college email verification and encourages affordable reuse.
- **Practical Revenue Model:** Charge a small optional promotion fee for listings and offer premium visibility to sellers. Basic buying and selling remain free.
- **Why It Is Achievable:** A student founder can begin with a simple website or WhatsApp-based pilot before developing a complete application.

**2. SkillSpark — Trade 30 Minutes, Learn Something New**

- **Problem:** Students want to learn practical skills such as video editing, public speaking, coding, or graphic design but may not be able to afford courses.
- **Surprising Solution:** A skill-exchange platform where students teach something they know in return for learning another skill. For example, a video editor can teach basic editing to a student who helps them improve their presentation skills.
- **Unique Selling Point:** Learning is based on exchanging knowledge rather than paying for every lesson.
- **Practical Revenue Model:** Offer optional paid workshops, verified skill badges, and premium mentor sessions.
- **Why It Is Achievable:** The first version can use a simple registration form, a spreadsheet for matching students, and scheduled online or offline sessions.

**3. Leftover Lab — Turn Food Waste into Shared Savings**

- **Problem:** Students living in flats often buy duplicate groceries, forget ingredients in the refrigerator, or waste food because they cannot plan meals efficiently.
- **Surprising Solution:** A shared grocery-planning system that lets flatmates list available ingredients, track expiry dates, and plan meals using food they already have.
- **Unique Selling Point:** Rather than simply recommending recipes, Leftover Lab focuses on reducing grocery expenses and preventing avoidable food waste among flatmates.
- **Practical Revenue Model:** Offer a premium meal-planning feature or partnerships with local grocery stores, with clear disclosure of sponsored recommendations.
- **Why It Is Achievable:** A student founder can test the concept with a shared spreadsheet or basic website before building automated features.

**Observation:**

The creative-tone prompt encouraged more memorable names, unconventional framing, and stronger differentiation. However, the ideas still address practical student problems and use low-cost approaches suitable for a student founder.

## Conclusion

This experiment demonstrated how prompt structure and tone influence AI-generated creative outputs. The base prompt produced practical startup ideas, while the professional prompt improved business-oriented structure and clarity. The creative prompt generated more memorable names and distinctive concepts, whereas the detailed prompt provided the most actionable information through target audiences, practical examples, estimated initial costs, and revenue models.

Overall, the experiment showed that the most effective prompt depends on the desired outcome. A combination of creative direction, clear structure, and specific requirements can produce outputs that are both original and useful. The ratings in this report are qualitative assessments based on the generated responses.

## Limitations

- Ratings are subjective and based on a small set of generated responses.
- Any cost estimates should be treated as estimates unless independently verified.
- Startup concepts should be validated with real students before business decisions are made.
